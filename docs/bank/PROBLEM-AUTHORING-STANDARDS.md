# Problem authoring and release standards

These are the standing rules for adding, revising, adapting, reviewing, and serving Calculus Ninja problems. They capture practices that worked in the bank and in the independent quality review. They apply to standalone items and bulk generators alike. Read them with [notation and worked solutions](NOTATION-AND-SOLUTIONS.md) and the [difficulty rubric](problem-quality/DIFFICULTY-AND-REVIEW.md).

This document is a maintained contract, not a claim that the current release has met every target. The [release status](problem-quality/STATUS.md) and exact evidence artifacts determine what is locally verified, imported, or live. AI review remains AI review; player calibration and human certification must never be implied.

## Design around a mathematical decision

1. **Describe the decision before writing variants.** Record what the solver must recognize, choose, justify, estimate, distinguish, or construct. Give the closest existing idea and explain the substantive difference. Count item records, construction families, related ideas, structural forms, and primary methods separately.
2. **Use purposeful finite cases.** Good changes exercise different hypotheses, boundary conditions, signs, geometric configurations, exceptional cases, or attainment mechanisms. Record why each case belongs. Weighted graph topologies, interior versus endpoint roots, and changing kernel sign patterns are useful examples from the current review. Their final admission still depends on the exact item review.
3. **Preserve mathematical lineage.** A renamed item, reordered expression, changed option list, or cosmetic revision must not masquerade as unseen mathematics. Carry aliases across revisions and topics. For advanced coverage, pure rescalings such as different amplitude/curvature budgets in the same normalized sharp inequality share a conservative capacity lineage. Document this qualification decision rather than treating raw variants as advanced headroom.
4. **Welcome elegant alternate solutions.** Symmetry, elimination, bounds, and elementary arguments are valid mathematics. For the reported symmetric integral, identifying the odd cubic and even cosine is clearer than exposing the builder parameter. A short solution can be deep; its length alone does not determine difficulty.
5. **Classify the displayed problem.** A reviewer solves with the actual four choices visible and records the easiest reasonable route. Move a routine construction to its honest level even when this reduces advanced coverage. When practical, withhold the intended method and proposed level until after the first solve; otherwise disclose that the review was not blind.

Elementary fluency may retain parameter-specific instances when the displayed numerical tasks require distinct calculations. They retain the same construction and idea group and contribute **zero new ideas**. This does not excuse exact duplicate tasks or cosmetic variants at any level. The advanced meaningful-capacity screen is deliberately stricter than counting ordinary fluency instances; [equivalence policy](problem-quality/equivalence-policy.json) records the distinction. An already-established alias survives reclassification, and baseline/candidate comparisons use the same mappings.

Historical integrity and active eligibility are separate. Preserve every historical identity, recorded answer, grade and replay. Active legacy content must earn its place through demonstrated mathematical correctness, useful practice, appropriate difficulty, clear explanations and fair options. Record the evidence for retaining a useful older construction, as well as the reason for repairing, reclassifying or withdrawing one. Age and bank size are not quality evidence. A configured heuristic that does not flag a family establishes only that bounded result; do not call every legacy family exploitable when the evidence shows a different weakness. Apply the same quality requirements to new content.

## Make every answer and explanation independently checkable

6. **Use a separate mathematical route.** The generator's returned answer is not its own certificate. Examples that worked include spanning-tree enumeration versus a grounded Laplacian, Legendre projection versus a monomial Gram inverse, an implicit characteristic-polynomial derivative versus eigenvector perturbation, and derivatives of the defining integrand versus an integrated Taylor coefficient. Check every admitted finite instance, not just one attractive example.
7. **Separate computation from proof obligations.** Exact algebra can verify an identity; convergence, interchange of operations, existence, uniqueness, branches, and sharpness need an explicit argument. State the hypotheses and connect them to this instance. Numerical agreement can supplement that argument, with its tolerance and scope recorded.
8. **Explain the decisive step and finish the derivation.** Start with the useful idea, show essential intermediate equations, justify the necessary conditions, and conclude with the verified choice. Define auxiliary symbols where introduced. Distinguish an attained maximum from a supremum approached by admissible functions.
9. **Review the literal statement.** Check parentheses, operator scope, quantifiers, interval orientation, summation indices, units, domains, and definitions. A CAS result or successful render does not establish that the displayed integrand says what the verifier intended. The missing parentheses around a Hankel weight in a candidate were caught this way and blocked admission.
10. **Bind the review to exact source.** Record constructor and review-code digests, raw item digest, finite domain, reviewer identity, evidence paths, and destination content hash. Unknown items and changed source fail closed. Preserve failed drafts and reviewer errors as evidence of what changed; do not replace a digest merely to silence a failing check.

Freeze the runtime source before exporting a review batch, then reconstruct the serialized items from that snapshot. This prevented moving-source review mismatches in the differentiation/limits workflow. If a later review changes a statement, issue a new snapshot and retain the old failure evidence. A registered exact polynomial constructor supplies mathematical verification only: standalone items also require a separate exact-hash editorial review entry before admission.

Keep review inventory separate from eligibility. A mathematically checked case may still be withdrawn for redundancy, poor options, or inappropriate classification. Apply per-item dispositions before counting capacity; the differentiation merge caught seven reviewed but explicitly withdrawn iterates. If frozen raw records omit editorial metadata already established separately, add an exact-source supplement with complete choice-ID coverage and a bound report. Never invent generic misconception labels or weaken the admission contract to make an export pass.

## Use options that test mathematics fairly

11. **Author misconception routes.** Give each wrong choice a concrete reason: a lost Jacobian, wrong endpoint, omitted integration factor, sign error, incorrect case, or indexing error. Check all six pairs for mathematical inequivalence and prove exactly one answer is correct on the stated domain. Check edge cases where mistakes coincide.
12. **Inspect the options as a set.** Compare sign, magnitude, divisibility, integer/fraction form, precision, length, simplification, units, and notation. Use comparable forms where justified; do not create a separate visual style for the correct answer. Keep fresh option shuffling and preserve one shared order for a competitive round.
13. **Run per-family, per-form screens with held-out cases.** Preserve the split and failures. An aggregate average cannot clear a family-specific tell. A one-item adaptation cannot supply a meaningful held-out accuracy estimate; perform a documented item-level comparison and state the limitation. A screen is a diagnostic, not automatic editorial approval.
14. **Let a shortcut change the review.** If a simple bound isolates an option, assess that actual route. The integration repair review found useful bounding shortcuts and used them to reconsider difficulty. A systematic arbitrary-offset or divisibility tell requires repair or withdrawal, not merely a lower selection weight.

Treat unresolved special-function identities explicitly. A symbolic engine's failure to simplify an expression, or a heuristic non-equality result, is not a sufficient mathematical argument. The complete series review found arctangent differences equal to single-arctangent wrong choices. Use the addition formula with its real branch and range, a separate analytic identity, or outward rational enclosures that actually separate each pair. Preserve the failed earlier scope and bind the stronger check to the repaired literal options. Numerical closeness alone proves neither equality nor inequality.

Review a repair for **new** clues as well as the original defect. Independent review of the negative-binomial repair removed a length cue, then caught that the correct fraction was the only rational cube in every case. Exact rational-square and rational-cube predictors now cover this class of clue, with a regression in `tests/option-audit.test.mjs`. A replacement needs competing plausible forms and a fresh complete-family screen; passing the original heuristic is insufficient. Check elementary bounds with the actual alternatives: the AGM candidate's monotone bounds eliminated every wrong choice without requiring its intended advanced method. That finding blocks its advanced classification until the choices or classification are repaired. Preserve the rejected drafts and the exact reason for each decision.

Inspect **relationships between choices**, too. A lone pair of opposite values can reveal the magnitude once the statement makes the sign obvious. The integration review found this in every logarithmic-moment instance even though the ordinary magnitude-rank predictor failed to replicate. Positive and negative opposite-pair predictors, with a regression, now cover this failure. Apply each new diagnostic to every effective family and answer form, including previously reviewed batches; preserve the earlier report and make any necessary immutable repairs. Do not treat a past local admission as permanent clearance under stronger evidence.

For scalar sign-repair pools, use either no opposite pair or two mathematically distinct opposite pairs when the misconception routes support that choice. Count **mathematical** opposites, including factored and expanded forms. A lone pair needs an explicit item-level disposition and complete-family evidence; its presence is a diagnostic, not a theorem that the question is defective. The independently checked 272-item ODE/multivariable repair batch meets the zero-or-two condition with all 1,632 literal pair inequalities checked. This establishes that finite repair property, not final catalog clearance. Unsupported tuple, categorical, or special-function syntax must receive a specific review; a parser failure never means “no clue.”

Use exact inequalities when they determine classification. For the control-energy cases, independently constructed Cauchy–Schwarz and feasible cubic bounds were checked against all literal choices using rational enclosures for pi and radicals. The resulting three Hard dispositions are bound in [the control review](problem-quality/control-energy-peer.json). This separates a demonstrated shortcut from a numerical guess.

Resolve review disagreements against the literal task. A failed independent check can expose a reviewer error as well as an author error. Preserve the failed run, identify which equation or assumption was misread, and rerun the corrected independent check without changing an already-correct problem to satisfy a mistaken oracle. The Riccati option review retained two such reviewer corrections before its complete passing run.

## Present mathematical language consistently

15. **Follow one notation policy throughout.** Use `\ln` for the natural logarithm, explicit bases for other logarithms, standard inverse-function forms, upright differentials, defined harmonic numbers, and stated real/complex branches. Consult primary mathematical references such as MathWorld, while acknowledging when several conventions are accepted. Apply the same policy to prompts, choices, intermediate steps, and conclusions.
16. **Keep prose responsive and mathematics intact.** Use `ProblemStatement` and `SolutionSteps`; keep short mathematical phrases inline and long defining equations in display blocks. Prose wraps as text. Complete fractions, cases, matrices, and paired delimiters remain intact. Wide equations scroll inside their card instead of widening the page. Avoid generator-preface text such as “Here m = 6.”
17. **Inspect the actual components.** Strictly render the complete inventory of new/revised expressions and their formatted fragments. Check short, long, piecewise, and special-function examples at desktop and mobile widths. Verify fonts load, no formula-source fallbacks appear, and content stays within its card. Keep screenshots and an honest account of which components and workflows were exercised.

Also inspect the resulting mathematical text: literal `quad`/`qquad` from a missing backslash and awkward double-negative tokens can render successfully while saying the wrong thing. The independent ODE/multivariable review found these in candidates despite a passing renderer. A second review caught a concrete parameter joined to `2^k`, displaying `42^k` where the derivation required `4\cdot2^k`. Put explicit multiplication between substituted numeric factors and inspect the resulting expression. Enclose every multidigit index or exponent in braces: the extension review caught `a_12`, which places only the first digit in the subscript; the intended coefficient is `a_{12}`. Missing spacing-command backslashes and unbraced multidigit scripts now fail admission. Rendering and semantic editorial inspection are separate requirements.

An extension must inherit the reviewed **meaning and presentation**, not blindly copy an old raw statement. Compare affected statements with their current source-bound presentation corrections, including explicit path exponents and derivative notation. Unwrap a prose layout only at that layout's own level: never globally replace TeX row separators, because nested matrices and piecewise definitions require them. The lower-extension review caught this distinction before admission; matrix and piecewise examples remain required visual checks after such a transformation.

Apply that inheritance rule to option-only revisions as well. A new content hash will not match an old presentation overlay. Resolve the current reviewed statement and worked solution against the exact old source, preserve that result in the new revision, then bind the new destination. The series option review detected historical builder prefaces in guarded source records before they could be mistaken for current displayed solutions.

## Preserve provenance and the player's history

18. **Record the origin truthfully.** Distinguish original mathematics with references, permitted adaptation, and reference-only research. Public availability is not permission. For an adaptation, record the exact item, edition, authors, exceptions, reuse terms, modification notice, and destination IDs. Retain item-scoped source/license credit in served content and exports; never relabel an adaptation as original or relicense unrelated application code.
19. **Preserve historical facts.** Meaning-preserving presentation corrections bind to the exact old source. Changed meaning, answer keys, difficulty, or parameter domains require immutable revisions. Keep prior hashes, saved questions, answers, grades, XP, ratings, and recorded replay explanations. Later explanation clarifications are separate from recorded playback.
20. **Model exposure honestly.** Issued, displayed, abandoned, and attempted questions have different evidential meaning. Date delayed displays from validated clock/server evidence, conservatively when evidence is missing. Receipt time must not refresh an old display. Displays never become accepted attempts or rewards. Test the actual game/replay API path, not only a simulation adapter.

## Protect sustained practice and controlled publication

21. **Keep the selector's useful randomness and constraints.** Normalize at the real family level; preserve fresh randomness and penalties for recent items, ideas, structures, and methods. Keep topic/difficulty/drill filters exact. Avoid a fixed method rotation. Competitive sets use common policy and saved shared order, not the host's personal exposure advantage.

Legacy practice follows the [optional legacy policy](problem-quality/LEGACY-POLICY.md): default off in Experimental settings, exact-revision quality and usefulness evidence before opt-in eligibility, and a visible Legacy marker. Default coverage and horizon gates must stand without it. The preference never overrides withdrawals or rewrites saved history. Preserve origin through revisions and renamed descendants, check it again after decoding, and verify evidence files at build and import time. Age alone neither qualifies nor disqualifies a mathematical idea.
22. **Measure the stated practice horizon.** Before simulation, calculate capacity after retirement, equivalence deduplication, and retained history using `sum(min(U_f, 2H)) >= 20H`, with about 20% headroom. Run the actual selector and displayed content with continuous attempted history. Use the [versioned session contract](problem-quality/session-contract.json), including all cells and Mixed pools, development and reserved seeds, zero repeats/duplicates/cap violations/adjacency/relaxation/request failures, and the qualified-idea floor. Report representative and worst sequences, concentration, recurrence, and resource bounds. Preserve failed seeds.
23. **Distinguish bounded sampling from true exhaustion.** Keep catalog, query, decode, and history limits explicit. Retrieve eligible unseen content through a bounded fallback before declaring exhaustion. Never truncate a large catalog silently. Test returning players, narrow drills, sparse pools, short rounds, mass retirement, and retention-bound histories. Explain legitimate narrow-pool limitations rather than silently widening a filter.
24. **Compose releases explicitly and preserve safety on rollback.** Flatten transitive sources, deduplicate memberships, prefer approved current revisions, export exclusions, and test repeated preparation/import. Declare global versus release-scoped retirement. Pointer rollback must not revive known-invalid content. Reconcile cumulative operation consumption, rehearse within the remaining allowance, verify before activation, and disable temporary import access afterward.
25. **Keep runtime authoring out of gameplay.** Author and review offline. Ordinary serving, selection, and grading make zero model calls. Protect solution keys until the proper answer/replay gate, while making required source and license notices accessible.

## Checklist for every future addition or revision

- [ ] Identify the mathematical decision, nearest neighbor, lineage, purposeful finite domain, and truthful source disposition.
- [ ] Independently solve the actual statement and choices; record the easiest route, alternate routes, proof obligations, disagreements, and final editorial level.
- [ ] Verify every admitted instance, intermediate claims, four distinct options, and one correct answer; resolve every option-screen flag with evidence.
- [ ] Apply notation and explanation rules, bind exact source/review/destination hashes, and reconstruct through the registered admission path.
- [ ] Run strict rendering and relevant actual-component visual checks, including loaded fonts and narrow widths.
- [ ] Update eligible coverage/equivalence counts and rerun affected session/regression gates without clearing attempted history or replacing failed seeds.
- [ ] Test immutable membership, historical resolution, exclusions and rollback; rehearse and publish through the existing verified, budgeted workflow.
- [ ] Record authored, verified, quarantined, imported, retired and active counts separately, and add any newly demonstrated reusable success to this guide with evidence.

For each completed release, add a short “practices retained” entry to its handoff: **observed success → rule to keep → evidence → regression/admission check**. Update this guide and the relevant executable contract together when a rule changes. Leave unresolved findings in the release status; never turn an aspiration into a claimed success.

Keep a running entry during development, too; see [this release's practices retained](problem-quality/PRACTICES-RETAINED.md). Record successes from rejected drafts when they demonstrate a useful review method, but label the rejected content and the method's exact scope. Future authors must consult both the standing rule and its evidence before reusing a construction.
