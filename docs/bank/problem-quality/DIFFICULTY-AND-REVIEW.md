# Editorial difficulty and independent review

This release uses AI-assisted authoring and a separate AI reviewer. It is not human certification or player calibration. Review the public statement **with its actual four options visible**, before consulting the intended method or proposed level. Record the easiest reasonable valid solution, including elimination, symmetry, bounds, and alternative advanced methods. Neither a named theorem nor a short solution determines a level on its own.

| Level | Expected decision and reasoning |
| --- | --- |
| Easy | A direct familiar rule or interpretation, with a clearly stated domain and little strategy selection. |
| Medium | Select a familiar representation or theorem and execute one or two connected steps; check a relevant endpoint, index, or domain condition. |
| Hard | A non-obvious transformation or combination of familiar ideas, a meaningful exceptional case, or an applicability argument requiring several dependent steps. Routine substitution into an advanced formula remains here when the options make the route immediate. |
| Olympiad | A substantial insight in selecting/reframing the problem, connecting ideas, proving an essential hypothesis, establishing sharpness, or controlling exceptional behavior. Advanced prerequisites are permitted when identified and necessary definitions are supplied. Large expressions, unfamiliar symbols, computational burden, and disguised coefficient variation do not establish this level. |

The reviewer records prerequisites, central decision, easiest options-visible route, hypotheses, essential reasoning depth, computational burden, alternate solutions, nearest-neighbor distinction, and disagreements. A conclusion-guessing conversion of a proof question does not preserve its original difficulty. Legitimate elimination is welcome; a reliable divisibility or formatting clue arising only from distractor construction is a defect.

An independent idea group requires a meaningfully different decision or argument from its nearest neighbors. Record counts, construction families, related ideas, structural forms, and primary methods are reported separately. Parameter changes and new option lists contribute instances, not ideas. A construction may have several useful small cases without claiming an unlimited supply of novelty. The same reviewed grouping is used for the deployed baseline and the candidate release; cosmetic revisions retain mathematical lineage.

Admission requires exact finite-domain reconstruction, independent checks of the answer and intermediate claims, proof/certificate review for non-computational obligations, four pairwise inequivalent choices, strict rendering, and a source-bound reviewer record. A computer identity check does not prove convergence, existence, uniqueness, interchange of limits, branch selection, or extremum attainment. Those obligations must be addressed explicitly in the review and worked solution. Unknown or unreviewed content fails closed.

Choice audits split finite instances into fixed development and held-out sets. They report each family and answer form separately and inspect answer rank/magnitude, sign, integer/fraction form, roundness/divisibility, precision, expression length, notation, and units. Small samples limit statistical certainty; a weak aggregate average cannot erase a family-specific tell. A flagged construction needs a documented mathematical explanation or revised choices/reclassification/withdrawal before admission. Generic additive offsets are not an acceptable repair.

Later calibration can use aggregate first attempts, with sample sizes, uncertainty, exposure history, repeated attempts, and selection effects disclosed. Until such evidence is collected and reviewed, the levels remain editorial. This release does not add ratings, a mastery model, reward changes, or live model calls.
