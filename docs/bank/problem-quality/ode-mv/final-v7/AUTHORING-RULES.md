# Rules established by the final ODE and multivariable review

These rules are supported by the source-bound review artifacts in this final review package and its history. They supplement the repository’s authoring standards; they do not claim a new independent solution of the full catalog.

1. Review what is actually served. Run the same fixed option diagnostic on immutable source records and on `presentBankProblem` output, including retained originals. Bind both menus to exact source hashes and preserve the raw report. The final1531 pass found 395 presentation changes and 16 records with choice-spelling changes.
2. Distinguish a diagnostic flag from its cause. Keep per-family and per-answer-form dispositions. A pooled average never clears a flagged family. For a repeated length signal, inspect literal ties and the mathematical source of the signal; a sign-pair structure, a numeric digit pattern, and a unique answer-format giveaway are different findings.
3. Try the shortest reasonable options-visible route before retaining a difficulty label. Increasing-density bounds uniquely select the current centroid menus, so their honest ceiling is Easy. A nonnormal norm menu that survives exact optimization can still fail elementary column and triangle bounds.
4. Removing one giveaway does not restore a higher difficulty automatically. Preserve earlier per-record Hard decisions unless an independent reassessment supports changing them. In this review, nonnormal indices 2/5/8 and control-energy indices 4/6/9 remain Hard.
5. A replacement distractor must be the actual result of a named mathematical mistake. For ground-state variance, reading the potential coefficient as a spatial variance parameter yields a specific wrong Gaussian and a specific wrong moment. Describe numerical factors precisely:2/a is four times1/(2a), not twice.
6. Check every finite replacement menu, not a representative example. Verify the correct answer independently, all six pairwise distinctions, and relevant opposite sums. Use exact algebra or outward rational certificates for the literal expressions. Floating-point checks may reveal a problem but do not close a near-equality question.
7. Recheck the full family after a local repair. A three-menu ground-state draft removed a length giveaway but created a 7/8 numerical-rank cue; it was rejected. The revised mistake preserves the original rank distribution while removing the unique-length clue. Do not optimize only the edited subset.
8. Keep rejected drafts and the reason they failed. Use a new frozen version for substantive changes and for corrections to bound authoring metadata. Do not rewrite historical raw constructors, source statements, or earlier failed evidence to make the final report look clean.
9. Preserve the full current solution when changing options or difficulty. Reuse the exact served explanation unless an independently checked mathematical correction is required. Match every new answer form to an algebraically equivalent conclusion, and render every statement, option, and worked step strictly.
10. Treat source identity, destination identity, and mathematical lineage separately. Bind each overlay to an exact source record; leave destination IDs and independent-review fields explicitly pending until supplied. Option spelling, distractor repair, and honest difficulty correction earn no new idea credit and retain the conservative normalized task key.
11. Completion requires exact destination checks. After admission, rebuild the global map and capacities from the final IDs, including retained originals; rerun the served option screen and preserve any residual limitations. A passing source-ID screen cannot substitute for this final check.

The completed portable overlay has 26 exact source, independent-review, and destination joins. Its final actual-ID screen and 15,877 strict rendered expressions are bound in this package. The amendment set is ten nonnormal menus, ten centroid difficulty corrections, and six ground-state revisions, of which three only correct inaccurate misconception descriptions. Earlier failed drafts remain in history.
